Methodology Note: Why Use Normalised Values?

The four variables displayed on each radar chart are measured in different units and have different numerical ranges:

  • Gradient: 0.58° to 7.16° (degrees)
  • Vegetation density: 33.8% to 45% (percentage cover)
  • People count: 1.0 to 7.16 (mean people count)
  • Infiltration rate: 36.51 to 99.31 (mm/hr)

Plotting these raw values together would create a misleading visual comparison, as variables with larger numerical values would appear to dominate the chart regardless of their actual significance.

Normalisation solves this problem by converting each variable to a common 0-100% scale. Each value is expressed as a percentage of the maximum value recorded for that variable across all three locations:

Normalised value = (Observed value ÷ Maximum value across all locations) × 100

For example, Location 2's infiltration rate of 99.31 mm/hr is the highest recorded, so it becomes 100%. Location 1's infiltration rate of 36.51 mm/hr becomes (36.51 ÷ 99.31) × 100 = 36.8%.

This transformation preserves the relative differences between locations while enabling direct visual comparison of variables that would otherwise be incompatible. It allows us to compare the "shape" of each location's profile and identify which variables are relatively high or low at each location.

Comparative Analysis

Location 1 Profile

Shows the smallest radar "footprint" overall. The flattest gradient (8% of max), lowest vegetation density (75% of max), and low people count (16% of max) combine to produce the lowest infiltration rate (37% of max). The compact shape suggests conditions less favourable for water infiltration.

Location 2 Profile

Displays a distinctive elongated shape with maximum gradient (100%) and maximum infiltration (100%), but minimum people count (14%). This profile suggests that low soil compaction from minimal foot traffic may be the key factor enabling high infiltration, despite—or perhaps because of—the steep slope.

Location 3 Profile

Shows maximum vegetation (100%) and maximum people count (100%), but only moderate infiltration (59% of max). This suggests the benefits of high vegetation cover may be partially negated by soil compaction from heavy foot traffic—a key finding for the hypothesis.
