Key Observations for Analysis

Notable Pattern: Location 2, with the steepest gradient (7.16°) and lowest people count (1.0), shows the highest mean infiltration rate (99.31 mm/hr) — nearly three times that of Location 1.

Location 1 exhibits the flattest terrain (0.58°) with the lowest vegetation density (33.8%) and moderate people count (1.16). Despite the gentle slope which might reduce surface runoff, this location has the lowest infiltration rate (36.51 mm/hr), possibly due to reduced vegetation cover limiting soil structure and organic matter.

Location 2 shows the steepest gradient (7.16°) with moderate vegetation (39.3%) and the lowest people count (1.0). Contrary to what might be expected, this location has the highest infiltration rate (99.31 mm/hr). The minimal soil compaction from low foot traffic, combined with reasonable vegetation cover, may create favourable conditions for water absorption despite the slope.

Location 3 combines a gentle slope (1.16°) with the highest vegetation density (45%) but significantly higher people count (7.16). The infiltration rate (58.72 mm/hr) falls between the other locations, suggesting the benefits of high vegetation may be partially offset by soil compaction from heavy foot traffic.

Hypothesis Testing: The data suggests people count may have a stronger negative impact on infiltration than gradient has a positive impact. Low people count at Location 2 correlates with high infiltration, while high people count at Location 3 correlates with reduced infiltration despite having the best vegetation cover.
