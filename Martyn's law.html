2. Legal Context and Overview

2.1 Purpose of the Legislation

The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025, commonly referred to as Martyn's Law, received Royal Assent on 3 April 2025. The Act is intended to improve protective security and organisational preparedness across the UK.

Implementation Timeline: The Government intends for there to be an implementation period of at least 24 months before the Act comes into force. This means the requirements are not yet legally enforceable but organisations should begin preparation.

2.2 Why This Matters

Since March 2017, security services and law enforcement have together disrupted 43 late-stage plots and there have been 15 domestic terror attacks. The Manchester Arena Inquiry and London Bridge Inquests called for the introduction of legislation and guidance to protect the public.

The threat level from terrorism in the UK is currently SUBSTANTIAL, meaning an attack is likely. Terrorists have targeted a wide range of people and places in recent years. It is not always possible to predict where an attack might happen, or the type of premises or events that could be impacted.

2.3 Newham Community Learning's Commitment

As the Trust Board of Newham Community Learning, we are committed to ensuring the health, safety and security of all children, young people, staff and visitors - as stated in our Health and Safety Principles Statement and Safeguarding Policy, both published on the Policies Page of the Trust website.

This policy sets out our approach to meeting the requirements of Martyn's Law across our family of schools.

3. Scope - Which Premises and Events are Covered

3.1 Qualifying Premises

Premises that satisfy the following four criteria fall within scope of the Act:

  1. There is at least one building on the premises (or the premises are in a building)
  2. The premises are wholly or mainly used for one or more of the uses specified in Schedule 1 to the Act (e.g. schools, entertainment venues, retail, hospitality)
  3. It is reasonable to expect that at least 200 individuals may be present at the same time, at least occasionally
  4. The premises are not excluded under Schedule 2 to the Act

The Trust Office on Exning Road is expected to fall below the 200-person threshold and therefore will not be a qualifying premises under the Act. However, we will apply best practice security principles to this location.

3.4 Qualifying Events

If any of our schools host qualifying events (events at premises that are not enhanced duty premises, with 800+ expected attendees, with entry controls, and accessible to the public), these will be subject to enhanced duty requirements for the duration of the event.

Examples might include:

  • Large-scale public performances or concerts
  • Community festivals held on school grounds
  • Large sporting events open to the public

4. Responsible Persons

4.1 Definition of Responsible Person

For qualifying premises, the responsible person is the person who has control of the premises in connection with their Schedule 1 use (education). For Newham Community Learning schools, the responsible person will typically be:

  • The Headteacher as the person in day-to-day control of the school premises
  • The Headteacher may delegate specific tasks but retains overall responsibility for compliance

4.2 Senior Individual Designation

For enhanced duty premises (our secondary schools, if they were subject to enhanced duty), where the responsible person is not an individual (i.e., the Trust as a corporate body), a senior individual must be designated. For Newham Community Learning:

Senior Individual for Enhanced Duty Premises: The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of Newham Community Learning will be designated as the senior individual with responsibility for ensuring the Trust (as the responsible person) complies with enhanced duty requirements across all secondary schools, should this be required for any qualifying events.

The CEO may designate this function to the Chief Operating Officer (COO) where appropriate.

  • The Trust Director of Safeguarding

5. Standard Duty Requirements (200-799 people)

5.1 Overview

All Newham Community Learning schools are subject to standard duty requirements under Martyn's Law. This applies to both our primary schools (which fall within the 200-799 threshold) and our secondary schools (which, despite having 800+ individuals, are treated as standard duty due to being educational settings).

5.2 Notification Requirement

The Headteacher (as responsible person) must notify the Security Industry Authority (SIA) that they are responsible for their premises. This notification will be completed:

  • When the Act comes into force
  • Using the SIA's designated notification system
  • With support from the Trust Central Team

5.3 Public Protection Procedures

The responsible person must have in place, so far as is reasonably practicable, appropriate public protection procedures. These are procedures to be followed by people working at the premises where they suspect an act of terrorism is occurring, or is about to occur, at the premises or in the immediate vicinity.

5.3.1 Types of Procedures

Public protection procedures may include:

  1. Evacuation procedures - clear routes and assembly points
  2. Invacuation (lockdown) procedures - securing the premises and keeping people safe inside
  3. Communication procedures - how to alert staff, students, and emergency services
  4. Procedures for securing entry/exit points - locking doors, closing shutters
  5. Procedures for directing people to safety - identifying safe areas and routes to cover

5.3.2 Integration with Existing Procedures

Schools already have extensive emergency procedures in place, including:

  • Fire evacuation procedures
  • Lockdown procedures (as part of safeguarding arrangements)
  • Critical incident management (as detailed in our Trust Critical Incident Guide)

Compliance with Martyn's Law will involve:

  • Reviewing existing procedures through a terrorism threat lens
  • Identifying any gaps or areas for enhancement
  • Ensuring procedures are documented and communicated
  • Regular testing and review

Key Point: The Act does not require physical alterations to premises or the purchase of equipment for standard duty premises. The focus is on procedures and staff preparedness.

5.4 Documentation

While standard duty premises are not required to provide documentation to the SIA, it is good practice to maintain records of:

  • The public protection procedures in place
  • Staff training and awareness sessions
  • Tests and drills conducted
  • Reviews and updates

6. Enhanced Duty Requirements (800+ people)

6.1 Overview

Enhanced duty requirements apply to premises where 800 or more individuals may reasonably be expected to be present at the same time, and to qualifying events. While our secondary schools exceed this threshold, they are treated as standard duty premises due to being educational settings.

This section is included for completeness and in case the Trust hosts or is involved with qualifying events that would be subject to enhanced duty requirements.

6.2 Enhanced Duty Requirements

If any of our premises or events were subject to enhanced duty, the requirements would include all standard duty requirements plus:

6.2.1 Public Protection Measures

In addition to procedures, the responsible person must have in place, so far as is reasonably practicable, appropriate public protection measures. These are measures that:

  • Reduce the vulnerability of the premises or event to acts of terrorism
  • Reduce the risk of physical harm to individuals if an attack were to occur

Examples might include:

  • CCTV or other monitoring systems
  • Access control measures (beyond basic locks)
  • Security personnel
  • Bag search policies
  • Vehicle checks or barriers
  • Physical protective measures (bollards, barriers)

6.2.2 Documentation and Submission to SIA

For enhanced duty premises or events, the responsible person must:

  • Document the public protection procedures and measures in place or proposed
  • Provide this documentation to the Security Industry Authority
  • Keep the documentation under review

6.2.3 Senior Individual

As noted in Section 4.2, where the responsible person is not an individual, a senior individual must be designated to ensure compliance.

7. Assessment and Planning

7.1 Capacity Assessment

Each school must undertake a reasonable assessment of the number of individuals who may be expected to be present at the same time, from time to time. This assessment will determine whether the premises are:

  • Below 200 individuals (not in scope)
  • 200-799 individuals (standard duty)
  • 800+ individuals (enhanced duty - though for schools, treated as standard duty)

7.1.1 Methods for Assessment

Schools may use various methods to make this assessment:

  1. Roll numbers plus staff - the total number of students on roll plus all staff who may be present
  2. Safe occupancy calculations - existing calculations used for fire safety purposes
  3. Historic attendance data - records of actual numbers present on typical days
  4. Event planning data - expected attendance at specific events

7.1.2 "From Time to Time"

The phrase "from time to time" reflects that premises may experience fluctuations. It refers to numbers that can be regularly expected, not one-off exceptional circumstances. For schools, this will typically be during term time on school days.

7.2 Risk Assessment

While not explicitly required by the Act, good practice involves conducting a risk assessment to inform the procedures and measures put in place. This should consider:

  • The nature and use of the premises
  • The location and surroundings
  • Known threats in the area
  • Potential attack methodologies
  • Vulnerability of the premises
  • Potential consequences of different attack scenarios

The Trust Director of Safeguarding will support schools in conducting these assessments, drawing on advice from the National Counter Terrorism Security Office (NaCTSO), local counter-terrorism advisors, and other expert sources.

7.3 Reasonably Practicable

All requirements relating to procedures and measures are subject to the concept of "reasonably practicable." This is a familiar concept from health and safety law.

What is Reasonably Practicable?

In determining what is reasonably practicable, the responsible person must take into account:

  • The particular circumstances of the premises or event
  • The nature of the premises or event
  • The resources available (financial, technical, personnel)
  • The degree of risk
  • The cost and effort required versus the reduction in risk achieved

What is reasonably practicable at one premises may not be at another. Procedures and measures should be tailored to the specific context.

8. Co-ordination and Co-operation

8.1 Co-ordination Requirements

If more than one person is responsible for qualifying premises or a qualifying event, they must, so far as is reasonably practicable, co-ordinate with each other in complying with the Act's requirements.

8.1.1 Scenarios Requiring Co-ordination

For Newham Community Learning schools, co-ordination may be required in scenarios such as:

  • Shared facilities: If a school shares facilities with another organisation (e.g., a sports hall used by both the school and a community club)
  • Multiple uses of premises: If different parts of a school building are used for different Schedule 1 purposes
  • Events on school grounds: If an external organisation hosts an event at a school

8.1.2 How to Co-ordinate

Co-ordination should involve:

  • Identifying who is responsible for which areas or activities
  • Sharing risk assessments and plans
  • Ensuring procedures are compatible and complementary
  • Agreeing communication protocols
  • Joint testing and review where appropriate

8.2 Co-operation Requirements

If a person has, to any extent, control of enhanced duty premises or premises where a qualifying event is to be held but is not the responsible person, they must, so far as is reasonably practicable, co-operate with the responsible person for the purposes of the responsible person complying with the Act.

8.2.1 Examples of Co-operation

This might include:

  • Landlords/freeholders: If a school leases its buildings, the freeholder may need to cooperate by considering requests for physical alterations
  • Facilities management companies: If premises management is contracted out, the contractor must cooperate with the responsible person
  • Shared service providers: If multiple schools share services, providers should cooperate with each school's requirements

8.3 Newham Community Learning Approach

As a Trust, we will:

  • Facilitate co-ordination between schools where they share facilities or hold joint events
  • Ensure the Trust Central Team co-operates with Headteachers (as responsible persons)
  • Work with our contractors and service providers to ensure they understand their co-operation duties
  • Establish clear protocols for who is responsible in different scenarios

9. Regulator and Compliance

9.1 The Regulator

The Security Industry Authority (SIA) is the regulator for the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act. The SIA will:

  • Provide advice and guidance to those responsible for premises and events
  • Receive notifications from responsible persons
  • Receive documentation from enhanced duty premises and qualifying events
  • Investigate suspected non-compliance
  • Take enforcement action where necessary

9.2 Enforcement Approach

The SIA's approach will be to support, advise, and guide those responsible for premises and events. Enforcement action will be reserved for cases of serious or persistent non-compliance.

9.2.1 Enforcement Powers

The SIA's enforcement toolkit includes:

  • Compliance notices: Requiring action to be taken within a specified timeframe
  • Monetary penalties: Fines for failing to fulfil requirements
  • Restriction notices: In the most serious cases, restricting use of enhanced duty premises or prohibiting qualifying events

There are also some criminal offences in the Act, including:

  • Failing to comply with a restriction notice
  • Providing false or misleading information to the SIA
  • Obstructing an inspector

9.3 Newham Community Learning Compliance Approach

Newham Community Learning is committed to full compliance with Martyn's Law. We will:

  1. Register with the SIA - Complete notifications for all qualifying premises within required timeframes
  2. Engage proactively - Seek guidance and advice from the SIA where needed
  3. Maintain documentation - Keep clear records of our assessments, procedures, and measures
  4. Respond to SIA communications - Address any concerns or requests from the SIA promptly
  5. Report critical incidents - Follow our Critical Incident Guide which includes appropriate reporting to authorities
  6. Learn and improve - Use feedback and lessons learned to continuously improve our approach

Board Oversight: As with critical incidents, the Trust Board will be informed of any SIA enforcement action. The COO will maintain a log of all SIA correspondence and any actions required.

10. Training and Awareness

10.1 Training Requirements

While the Act does not prescribe specific training requirements, it is essential that those with responsibility for carrying out public protection procedures and measures are appropriately instructed and, as needed, trained.

10.2 Newham Community Learning Training Programme

10.2.1 Senior Leadership Training

Audience: CEO, COO, Headteachers, Deputy Headteachers, School Business Managers

Content:

  • Overview of Martyn's Law requirements
  • Understanding responsibilities as "responsible persons"
  • Conducting capacity assessments
  • Risk assessment principles
  • Developing and documenting procedures and measures
  • Co-ordination and co-operation requirements
  • SIA notification and compliance

10.2.2 All Staff Training

Audience: All staff at qualifying premises

Content:

  • Awareness of terrorism threats and attack methodologies
  • Run, Hide, Tell principles
  • School-specific evacuation and invacuation procedures
  • How to recognise and report suspicious behaviour
  • Communication protocols in an emergency
  • Supporting students during and after an incident

10.2.3 Specialist Training

Audience: Reception staff, site managers, security personnel

Content:

  • Access control procedures
  • Visitor management
  • Monitoring and surveillance
  • Incident response and first action
  • Liaison with emergency services

10.3 Training Delivery

Training will be delivered through:

  • ACT Awareness e-learning: Free online training from ProtectUK on recognising and responding to terrorism
  • In-house training sessions: Delivered by the Trust Director of Safeguarding and school DSLs
  • External training providers: Specialist training from NaCTSO, local counter-terrorism advisors, or approved providers
  • Staff induction: All new staff will receive terrorism awareness training as part of their induction
  • Refresher training: Annual refresher for all staff
  • Testing and drills: Regular testing of procedures (see section 11.2)

10.4 Student Awareness

Age-appropriate awareness will be provided to students through:

  • PSHE curriculum
  • Assemblies
  • Participation in evacuation and lockdown drills
  • Discussions as part of safeguarding education

The approach will be reassuring rather than frightening, focusing on empowering students to keep themselves and others safe.

11. Review and Monitoring

11.1 Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed:

  • Annually, or sooner if there are significant changes to the legislation or statutory guidance
  • After any critical incident or major security event at a Trust school
  • Following any enforcement action by the SIA
  • As part of the Trust's policy review schedule

11.2 Testing and Exercising

Each school will test its public protection procedures at least termly through:

  • Evacuation drills: Testing fire evacuation procedures (which also serve terrorism response purposes)
  • Lockdown drills: Testing invacuation procedures
  • Desktop exercises: Senior leadership team working through scenarios
  • Full exercises: Multi-agency exercises involving police and other emergency services where possible

All tests and exercises will be:

  • Documented with learning points identified
  • Used to refine and improve procedures
  • Reported to the Trust Board annually

11.3 Monitoring and Reporting

11.3.1 School-Level Monitoring

Each Headteacher (as responsible person) will:

  • Conduct regular reviews of procedures and measures
  • Monitor staff training completion rates
  • Review outcomes of tests and drills
  • Keep documentation up to date
  • Report to the Local Governing Body on compliance

11.3.2 Trust-Level Monitoring

The Trust Central Team will:

  • Maintain an overview of compliance across all schools
  • Provide support and guidance to schools as needed
  • Facilitate sharing of good practice
  • Coordinate with the SIA on behalf of schools
  • Maintain the Trust's Martyn's Law compliance log
  • Report annually to the Trust Board on Martyn's Law compliance

11.3.3 Trust Board Oversight

The Trust Board will:

  • Receive an annual report on Martyn's Law compliance
  • Be immediately informed of any serious incidents or SIA enforcement action
  • Approve this policy and any major revisions
  • Ensure adequate resources are allocated to meet requirements

11.4 Continuous Improvement

Newham Community Learning is committed to continuous improvement in our approach to protective security. We will:

  • Learn from incidents and near-misses at our schools and elsewhere
  • Stay informed of emerging threats and best practice
  • Engage with NaCTSO, local counter-terrorism advisors, and peer networks
  • Update our procedures and measures in light of experience and guidance
  • Foster a culture where security and preparedness are everyone's responsibility

3. Immediate Actions on Suspicion of Terrorist Attack

3.1 Run, Hide, Tell

In the event of a terrorist incident, staff should follow the nationally recognised "Run, Hide, Tell" principles:

  • RUN - If there is a safe route, run to a place of safety. Encourage others to run with you, but don't wait for others. Leave belongings behind.
  • HIDE - If you can't run, hide. Find cover from gunfire, lock yourself in, barricade doors, stay quiet, silence mobile phones.
  • TELL - When safe to do so, call 999 (or 112) and provide location, direction, descriptions, casualties, and your contact details.

3.2 Lockdown (Invacuation) Procedure

Signal: [Specify signal - e.g., "Specific announcement over PA" / "Coded message" / "Alert app notification"]

Actions:

  1. Staff in classrooms: Secure classroom doors, move students away from windows, keep students calm and quiet
  2. Staff in corridors or common areas: Move to nearest safe room, assist others to do so, then secure the room
  3. Reception/Office staff: Secure main entrance, call 999, initiate emergency contact cascade
  4. Site staff: Secure perimeter gates if safe to do so, then move to safety
  5. All staff: Turn mobile phones to silent (not off), wait for further instructions, do not attempt to evacuate unless directed

5. Specific Scenarios

5.1 Suspicious Package

  • Do not touch or move the package
  • Evacuate the immediate area
  • Call 999
  • Inform senior leadership
  • Be prepared to evacuate wider area if directed

5.2 Suspicious Individual on Site

  • Challenge politely if safe to do so: "Can I help you?"
  • If behaviour is concerning, alert senior leadership immediately
  • Do not confront if person appears threatening
  • Call 999 if there is an immediate threat
  • Be prepared to implement lockdown

5.3 Incident in the Vicinity

  • If aware of an incident nearby (e.g., attack at a neighbouring location), implement lockdown
  • Monitor news and police communications
  • Follow instructions from emergency services
  • Be prepared to receive evacuees from other locations

  • Conduct debrief and review
  • Update procedures based on lessons learned

Annex D: Glossary of Key Terms

Term Definition
Act of Terrorism Has the meaning given by section 1 of the Terrorism Act 2000 - the use or threat of action designed to influence government or intimidate the public for the purpose of advancing a political, religious, racial or ideological cause
Enhanced Duty Premises Qualifying premises where 800 or more individuals may be reasonably expected to be present at the same time (though for educational premises, enhanced duty requirements do not apply)
Invacuation Securing people safely inside a building (also known as lockdown)
NaCTSO National Counter Terrorism Security Office - provides protective security advice
Public Protection Measures Measures that could reasonably be expected to reduce (i) the vulnerability of premises/event to acts of terrorism, and (ii) the risk of physical harm to individuals if an attack occurred
Public Protection Procedures Procedures to be followed by people working at premises where they suspect an act of terrorism is occurring or about to occur at the premises or in the immediate vicinity
Qualifying Event An event at premises meeting specific criteria including 800+ expected attendees, entry controls, and public accessibility
