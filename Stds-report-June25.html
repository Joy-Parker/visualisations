⚖️ Gender Context: Important Nuance

  • National pattern (2022/23): Girls average +0.12 Progress 8, boys average -0.17 (0.29 gap)
  • Sarah Bonnell (girls only): +0.46 is exceptional even for girls' schools, far exceeding national average
  • Rokeby (boys only): +0.06 is outstanding for boys, well above national boys' average of -0.17
  • Mixed schools consideration: Eastlea and Lister underperforming for both genders based on national averages
  • Key takeaway: While gender patterns exist nationally, both single-sex schools exceed expectations for their demographics, suggesting strong practices that mixed schools could learn from

📌 Executive Summary

  • NCL serves 5,071 students across highly diverse communities (avg 68% EAL, 50% PP)
  • Proof of concept: Both single-sex schools (Sarah Bonnell girls, Rokeby boys) significantly outperform national averages for their gender, demonstrating disadvantage and EAL are NOT barriers with right support
  • Gender context matters: Nationally, girls outperform boys by 0.29 in Progress 8, but both NCL single-sex schools exceed their gender norms
  • Critical issues: SEND exclusion disparity (2.2x), attendance crisis (no schools meeting targets), two mixed secondaries underperforming for both genders
  • Blueprint exists: Common success factors across Sarah Bonnell and Rokeby (high attendance, low PA, strong inclusion) can be scaled to mixed schools
  • Urgent action needed: Eastlea transformation, SEND inclusion practices, attendance interventions, learning from single-sex success models
  • Key lever: Attendance is the foundation - every 1% improvement correlates with better outcomes regardless of gender composition
